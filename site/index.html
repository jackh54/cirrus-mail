Three steps from domain to inbox.

Everything lives in your Cloudflare account. Mail comes in through Email Routing, goes out through Email Sending, and a single Worker does the rest.

  1. Onboard your domain

    In the Cloudflare dashboard, onboard your domain to Email Sending, which adds SPF, DKIM and a bounce MX. Then enable Email Routing. A DMARC record is recommended.

  2. Deploy the Worker

    Use the Deploy to Cloudflare button, or npm run deploy from the CLI. D1 and R2 are provisioned for you, and migrations are applied.

  3. Create your mailboxes

    Open /setup to create the admin account, add your domain and mailboxes, then point the catch-all routing rule at cirrus-mail.

Deploy from the CLI
$ git clone https://github.com/jackh54/cirrus-mail.git && cd cirrus-mail
$ npm ci && npx wrangler login
$ npx wrangler secret put SETUP_TOKEN
$ npx wrangler secret put APP_SECRET
$ npm run deploy   # checks, builds, migrates D1, deploys

Everything you expect from a modern mail client.

Built for a person, a family or a small team. No bulk mail, no tracking, no ads.

Real conversations

Messages thread by their headers, with a subject fallback, so replies land where they belong.

Fast full-text search

Search with D1 full-text search plus from:, to:, has:attachment and is:unread.

A compose window you'll like

Rich text, recipient chips with autocomplete, send-as aliases, drag-and-drop attachments and autosave.

Send later, undo send

Schedule a message for tomorrow morning, or take it back in the five seconds after you hit send.

Filters and spam scoring

Rules that move, star, mark read or forward. Spam is scored from SPF, DKIM and DMARC results, plus your blocklist.

Labels, snooze and mute

Colour-coded labels, snooze a conversation until later, mute noisy threads, and unsubscribe from newsletters in one click.

Shared mailboxes

Give support@ or hello@ several members. Everyone can read and reply from the same mailbox.

Aliases and catch-alls

Extra addresses for a mailbox, and a per-domain catch-all for everything else.

Vacation replies

Date-ranged auto-replies that skip mailing lists and bulk mail, and reply to each sender only once every few days.

Works on your phone

A responsive layout, installable as a PWA, with light and dark themes and new-mail notifications.

Keyboard first

c to compose, / to search, j/k to move, e to archive, r to reply. Press ? for the rest.

Calendar and contacts

Calendar and Contacts pages with invite cards, plus CalDAV and CardDAV sync for Apple and Android using app passwords.

A JMAP server

IMAP can't run on Workers, so mail apps connect over JMAP instead. It hasn't been tried with a third-party client yet.

Optional PGP

Encrypt, sign and verify in your browser. The server never sees your passphrase or an unencrypted private key.

Import from Gmail

Bring your old mail over from a Google Takeout .mbox file, labels and stars included. Export to mbox any time.

iOS app, in beta

A native iPhone app that talks to your server over JMAP. It's an early beta on TestFlight, and needs iOS 26 or later.

Admin console

Manage users, domains, mailboxes and aliases. A DNS check for MX, SPF and DMARC, and usage stats.

One Worker, your account

No Durable Objects, no Queues and no third-party services. Upgrade with git pull && npm run deploy.

Acceptable use
Cloudflare documents Email Sending as being for transactional email. Its documentation doesn't explicitly address person-to-person mail from your own mailbox, which is what Cirrus does. Cirrus has no bulk or marketing features and never will. Review Cloudflare's terms for your use.

Your mail, locked down by default.

Email is the key to every other account you own, so Cirrus treats it that way.

  • Passwords, 2FA and passkeysPBKDF2-SHA256 hashing, optional TOTP with hashed recovery codes, passkey sign-in, and login throttling.
  • Sandboxed HTML mailSanitized with DOMPurify and rendered in a sandboxed frame with no scripts and a strict CSP.
  • Remote images blockedTrackers don't load. When you allow images, they're fetched through a signed proxy on your own Worker.
  • Signed attachment linksAttachment URLs are HMAC-signed and expire after an hour.
  • No invocation logsRequest logs are off, and message content is never logged.

Questions, answered honestly.

What do I need to run it?

A Cloudflare account on Workers Paid, and a domain whose DNS is on Cloudflare. Use the apex domain (example.com) for mail, because catch-all routing works on apex domains only. The setup guide walks through the rest.

Is person-to-person email allowed on Email Sending?

Cloudflare documents Email Sending as being for transactional email, and doesn't explicitly address mail people write from their own mailbox. Cirrus sends one human-written message at a time, to at most 50 recipients, and has no bulk or marketing features. Whether your use fits is between you and Cloudflare, so review their terms first.

Can I use Apple Mail, Outlook or Thunderbird?

Not over IMAP, and that can't change inside Cirrus: Cloudflare Workers can't accept inbound TCP connections. Cirrus has a JMAP server instead, which JMAP-capable apps can use with an app password, though it hasn't been tried with a third-party client yet. Calendars and contacts sync over CalDAV and CardDAV. The web client installs as a PWA, and there's an iOS app in TestFlight beta.

Where is my mail stored, and who can read it?

In your own Cloudflare account: metadata and the search index in D1, and raw messages and attachments in R2. There's no Cirrus server and no third-party service. Mail isn't end-to-end encrypted, and Cloudflare's terms and privacy policy apply to your account.

Can I host more than one domain, or share a mailbox?

Yes to both. Add as many domains as you like in Admin, each onboarded to Email Sending and Email Routing. Any mailbox can have several members, which makes it a shared mailbox.

How do I upgrade and back up?

Upgrade with git pull && npm run deploy, which applies new D1 migrations first. Back up with wrangler d1 export for the database, and any S3-compatible tool for the R2 bucket.

Is it production-ready?

It's a beta (v0.1), and Email Sending has been in public beta since April 2026. The automated tests run in the Workers runtime, but some screens, such as Calendar, Contacts and passkeys, haven't been exercised in a live browser, and mbox import hasn't run against a multi-GB Takeout on a deployed Worker. Test it on a spare domain before you move your main mail over.
